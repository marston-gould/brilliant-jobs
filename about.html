Why Brilliant Jobs Exists

Marston
Founder & CPO

My name is Marston. This past year I turned 60, traveled to Naples, Capri, and the Amalfi Coast — and came home to find myself out of a job. Again.

The company I was working for had been acquired. It wasn't the first time. Over a career spanning three decades, I've changed jobs more times than I ever intended to. Some of those moves were my choice. Some weren't. A few of those roles I took simply because I needed to — bills don't negotiate. But there were others I wanted to last. Those endings hit differently.

What I noticed, every single time, is how broken the job search has become. The listings that lead nowhere. The applications that vanish into silence. The roles that were never really open to begin with. The platforms that pretend to help you while doing very little of either.

It gets harder the more senior you are. And I'll say what a lot of people my age won't: it gets harder when you're 60. At some point the responses stop coming, and you're left wondering whether you're overqualified, over the hill, or just invisible. Probably some combination of all three.

Being 60 also gives you time to reflect on the career itself. I've made mistakes — I'm sure most people have. I've also worked for companies where my opinions didn't matter and my ideas weren't wanted. Places where the expectation was simply to put your head down and execute. That never sat well with me.

So when the last acquisition landed and the usual options felt either closed or beneath me, I made a different choice. I decided to build.

Brilliant Jobs is the first of several ideas I've been sitting on. I poured 30 years of digital expertise into it — and then, starting in mid-February, taught myself to build it entirely through AI, vibe coding my way through Claude with no idea what I was doing and no intention of stopping. What you're looking at is the result of that.

I hope you find it useful. But if you don't — if something's broken, missing, or just wrong — please tell me. I will read every comment, every idea, and every piece of feedback myself. Personally.

One last thing. If you're here, you're probably in the middle of something hard. My only advice: don't be hard on yourself. You are brilliant. You deserve to be recognized for what makes you special.

That's the whole reason this exists.

— Marston
